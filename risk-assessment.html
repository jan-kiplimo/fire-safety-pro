Fire Risk Assessment

A fire risk assessment is the process of identifying potential fire hazards, considering who could be harmed, and determining suitable measures to reduce fire risks in a building or workplace.

What Hazards Should Be Identified?

  • Electrical hazards: Damaged wiring, overloaded sockets, and faulty electrical equipment.
  • Ignition sources: Open flames, hot surfaces, and equipment that could start a fire.
  • Combustible materials: Paper, fabrics, packaging, and improperly stored flammable substances.
  • Fire protection: The condition and accessibility of fire alarms and extinguishers.
  • Escape routes: Blocked exits, inadequate signage, and obstacles along evacuation routes.

Steps in a Fire Risk Assessment

  1. Identify potential fire hazards.
  2. Identify people who could be at risk.
  3. Evaluate the risks and existing safety measures.
  4. Recommend suitable precautions and improvements.
  5. Record findings and assign corrective actions.
  6. Review the assessment when conditions change.

Why Is Fire Risk Assessment Important?

Assessments help organizations understand fire hazards, protect occupants, reduce property damage, improve emergency planning, and address applicable fire safety requirements.

Reducing Fire Risks

  • Keep emergency exits and escape routes clear.
  • Maintain fire detection and alarm systems.
  • Store flammable materials safely.
  • Arrange appropriate fire safety training.
  • Inspect and maintain safety equipment.

Important: A competent person should conduct the assessment and recommend precautions appropriate to the building and applicable fire safety requirements.
